## (b) Paradigms

### Market

- **How it works:**
  - Prices do two jobs, a "signalling function" and a "rationing function" (Economic calculation problem).
  - Property rights do not require private ownership (Market economy).
  - The state can still set price ceilings and floors and Pigovian taxes (Market economy).
- **Where it works:** dispersed, "tacit, constantly changing" knowledge is used without anyone collecting it (Hayek, in Economic calculation problem).
- **Where it fails:**
  - Efficiency rests on "perfect and costless information", and "nonconvexities are pervasive" (Stiglitz, in Market economy).
  - Prices are "administered prices" set by "quasi monopolies" (Joan Robinson, in Economic calculation problem).
  - "markets inherently produce class division" (Albert and Hahnel, in Market economy).
  - There is "no natural or automatic tendency towards full employment" (Post-Keynesian economics).
  - Credit at "artificially low interest rates" leads to "malinvestment" and then a bust (Austrian school).

### Planned (command)

- **How it works:**
  - Material balances are kept in natural units. Each round, "deficits signaled the need for extra output in the successive iteration" (Material balance planning).
  - Gosplan balanced 1,943 items in 1973, about 70% of industrial output. Consumer goods and labour were left to markets (Material balance planning).
- **Where it works:**
  - It can force investment. The USSR cut consumption from about 80% to 50% of output to build heavy industry, while agriculture shrank (Planned economy).
  - "The static state can dispense with economic calculation" (Mises, quoted in Economic calculation problem), so planning is easiest when the economy is stable.
- **Where it fails:**
  - Capital goods become "merely internal transfers", so they have no prices. Mises called rational activity under these conditions "impossible" (Economic calculation problem).
  - Caplan's reply: socialism is less efficient, not "impossible", and the real failure was incentives (Economic calculation problem).
  - "endemic supply problems stemming from the crudity of the material balance technique, where balances were highly aggregated" (Material balance planning).
  - "unrealistic targets, bureaucratic waste, bottlenecks and shortages". Targets set "in terms of physical quantity of output" hurt quality (Planned economy).
  - Eastern Bloc economies showed "greater fluctuations in output than market economies" (Planned economy).
  - Under the soft budget constraint, bailouts, soft credit and soft taxes from a "paternalistic" state remove the penalty for losses (Kornai, in Budget constraint).
  - "chronic shortage economies" with second economies, that is black markets (Kornai, in Marxian economics).

### Mixed

- **How it works:**
  - Private firms predominate and the state regulates. Public ownership is about 15–20% (Mixed economy).
  - Three variants: markets plus intervention; private plus public firms; markets plus planning (Mixed economy).
- **Disputed:**
  - Mises held that no stable mixture exists, because state firms stay under "market sovereignty" (Mixed economy).
  - Marxists hold that either the law of value or planning dominates (Mixed economy).
- **Variant, market socialism:**
  - Lange's board sets prices by trial and error with P=MC, a form of "market simulated planning". Critics called it "capitalism without capital markets" (Socialist calculation debate; Market socialism).

### Feudal (manorial)

- **How it works:**
  - Dues were paid "with labour time or in-kind produce at first, and later by cash payment" (Manorialism).
  - Land was demesne (about a third of arable), villein holdings or free land.
  - The lord held banalities: mill, oven, wine-press, pannage, court fees and succession fines.
  - Tenure became "almost universally hereditary" (Manorialism).
- **Where it works:** it is flexible. It "could maintain a warrior, but it could equally well maintain a capitalist landlord" (Andrew Jones, in Manorialism).
- **Where it fails, or changes shape:**
  - Peasants could not leave "until demographic and economic circumstances made flight a viable proposition" (Manorialism).
  - Labour dues were commuted to cash from the 13th century. After inflation, lords re-imposed labour dues "as the value of fixed cash payments declined in real terms" (Manorialism).
  - Distant holdings paid cash; pastoral uplands, needing less labour, had freer peasants.

### Distributist / needs-based (in kind)

- **Name clash:** Wikipedia's "Distributism" is a different system: productive property "widely owned rather than concentrated", with small proprietors, guilds and cooperatives. It holds capitalism and state socialism to be "equally flawed". Its subsidiarity principle says "no larger unit … should perform a function that a smaller unit can perform" (Distributism). The user's duty-filled common stores match instead [inference]:
  - "calculation in kind" (Economic calculation problem);
  - Neurath's moneyless accounting in natural units (Socialist calculation debate);
  - "The redistribution economy, which is a more authoritarian case of mutualism. For example, the Incas" (Non-monetary economy).
- **Where it works:**
  - Calculation in kind can run as a decentralised "self-regulating system of stock control" (Robin Cox, in Economic calculation problem).
  - Neurath and Ticktin argued that real-unit accounting plus demand surveys works when goods are plentiful (Economic calculation problem).
  - Gift norms make hoarding shameful: the hoarder in The Flats was "alienated" from the community (Gift economy).
- **Where it fails:**
  - Mises's wine-or-oil example: choosing the end is easy, but "the bewildering mass of intermediate products" is not (Economic calculation problem).
  - Nove estimated the calculations "would take millions of years" (Economic calculation problem).
- **Stakhanovite variant:**
  - Norms were broken, for example at 184% of quota. The movement was "Initially popular", then met resistance as demands became "unrealistic". Objectors were branded "wreckers" (Stakhanovite movement).
  - The real gains came from "better organization of workflow processes … specialization and better task sequencing" (Stakhanovite movement).
- **Gift and prestige variant:**
  - The delay between gift and counter-gift keeps one side in debt (Gregory, in Gift economy).
  - In moka, status comes from the increment over what was received. Toraja gift contests convert into land and rank (Gift economy).
  - Among the Tiv, three spheres of exchange each had their own money. Universal money "damaged established social relationships" (Bohannan, in Gift economy).

### Cooperative / participatory

- **How it works:**
  - Workers' and consumers' councils. Say in a decision is proportional to how much it affects you.
  - Balanced job complexes. Pay for "effort and sacrifice".
  - Iteration Facilitation Boards publish indicative prices each round and have no power to decide (Participatory economics).
  - Worker self-management (Economic democracy).
- **Where it works:**
  - Hahnel calls it "incentive compatible", and it treats externalities as "the rule rather than the exception" (Participatory economics).
  - Mondragon and Legacoop reached "significant scale" and "long-term sustainability" (Economic democracy).
- **Where it fails:**
  - It becomes "obsessed with comparison … monitoring … details of consumption". Peer review lets people slack (Schweickart, in Participatory economics).
  - Sacrifice cannot be observed (Burczak, in Participatory economics).
  - "people's daily needs cannot be established well in advance simply by asking people what they want" (Participatory economics).
  - Critics on the left warn of a coordinator class, "coordinatorism" (Participatory economics).

### Physiocracy and mercantilism (useful presets)

- **Mercantilism:** "buy no more from strangers than we sell them". Wealth is bullion. Import bans and export bans on bullion (Mercantilism).
- **Physiocracy:** Quesnay's Tableau has three classes: proprietary, productive (farmers) and "sterile" (artisans and merchants). Only land yields a surplus (Physiocracy).
- **Georgism:** tax the "unimproved land value" and leave land in private hands. "socially created wealth in land values" is privatised (Georgism).
- **Chartalism:** fiat money "has value because governments impose taxes that must be paid in the currency" (Chartalism).

## (c) Mainstream assumptions the articles say are contested

1. **Equilibrium exists and is reached.** Stiglitz, Nove and Kornai say it exists for neither plan nor market (Socialist calculation debate); so do Cottrell and Cockshott (Economic calculation problem), and Austrians and post-Keynesians (Heterodox economics).
2. **Flexible prices clear markets.** Post-Keynesians warn of the "possible negative impact of flexible prices".
3. **Money is exogenous and neutral, and grew out of barter.** Disputed by post-Keynesian endogenous money, Chartalist tax-driven money, the Austrian credit cycle, and the anthropology of spheres of exchange (Gift economy).
4. **Rational homo economicus.** Agents use "repertoires of many ready-made rules" (Shiozawa, in Heterodox economics); preferences are manufactured by firms, Galbraith's "dependence effect" (Institutional economics).
5. **Markets are efficient.** Disputed by Stiglitz (information, nonconvexity), Hahnel (externalities), Robinson (administered prices).
6. **Planning is impossible.** Caplan says "inefficient", not "impossible"; Cockshott and Cottrell point to computers; the "Walmart" argument says firms already plan internally. The counter is that Big Data only knows the past (Economic calculation problem).

## (d) Mechanics the engine can simulate

- **Planner error:** error grows with the number of goods and with aggregation (Material balance planning); data arrive turns late (Lavoie); N balancing passes per turn, N set by admin capacity, deficits carried forward [inference on N].
- **Misreporting:** managers understate capacity for easy targets (Hayek: an "incentive to misreport"); the planner sees only reports.
- **Physical-quantity targets:** producers meet the quantity and let quality drift down (Planned economy).
- **Soft budget constraint:** bailouts, then cost growth and input hoarding, then shortage; a black market grows with the shortage (Kornai).
- **Lange mode:** the planner raises the price of whatever ran short and lowers the price of whatever piled up, each turn. This gives a market-like result without private capital.
- **Forced accumulation slider:** the consumption share can be pushed down to fund heavy industry, at the cost of farm output and morale (Planned economy).
- **Stakhanovite emulation:** a record ratchets everyone's norm; a short boost, then fatigue and resistance; helpers' output is credited to the hero (Stakhanov's helpers, 1988 press); the lasting gain is only better task sequencing.
- **Parecon:** meeting time costs labour; effort scores are noisy; peer review allows slack; wants are filed in advance and miss when they change.
- **Manorial dues:** lords pick labour, kind or cash; inflation erodes fixed cash rents, so labour dues return; flight only when labour is scarce (after a plague, say); distance pushes dues toward cash.
- **Gift and prestige:** the increment over a gift buys rank; an unreturned gift binds as debt; hoarding costs standing; universal money breaks the spheres of exchange.
- **Austrian cycle:** cheap credit funds long, "roundabout" projects. When rates normalise they are abandoned half-built.
- **Effective demand:** when wages or orders fall, demand falls and unemployment persists. There is no automatic recovery.
- **Chartalist money:** a currency the state takes for taxes gains demand. Untaxed regions keep barter (Russia in the 1990s ran about 50% barter under tight money; Non-monetary economy).
- **Ceilings and floors:** price ceilings cause shortages and queues; price floors cause surpluses (Market economy).
- **Georgist land value tax:** a tax on land rent that does not reduce output [inference: from the claim that land is "inherently limited in supply"].
