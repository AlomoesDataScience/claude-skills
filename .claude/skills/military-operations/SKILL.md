# Military operations (FM 3-0) for games

Source: `Military Theory/ARN43326-FM_3-0-000-WEB-1.pdf` (+ `.txt`, "=== page N" = PDF page),
21 March 2025 edition. Claude's own summary; PDF pages in brackets. Tactics (how one battle is
fought) live in **military-doctrine**; the Soviet school in **soviet-doctrine**.

## Levels of war [26-28]
- **Strategic** - policy aims and the instruments of national power (in games: the whole-war
  choices - who to fight, economy, alliances).
- **Operational** - campaigns and major operations that link battles to strategic aims (in games:
  a front, a theatre, a season's offensive, a 4X war across systems).
- **Tactical** - battles (sets of related engagements) and engagements (in games: one battle map).
- A game turn often spans two levels: plan the operational move first, then each battle.

## Principles of war [table 1-1, appendix A 219-224] - a check, not a checklist
Objective (every action toward a clear, decisive, reachable goal) - Offensive (seize, keep and
exploit the initiative; even in defense think attack) - Mass (concentrate effects at the best place
and time) - Economy of force (minimum on secondary efforts so the main effort gets the maximum) -
Maneuver (put the enemy at a positional disadvantage at least cost) - Unity of command - Security
(deny them surprise) - Surprise (time, place or manner they are not ready for; show them what they
expect until the last moment, then do something else) - Simplicity (a simple plan on time beats a
clever one late). Joint additions: restraint, perseverance, legitimacy.

## Tenets [63-69] - build them into every plan
- **Agility** - act and react faster than the enemy (tempo = relative speed and rhythm vs the enemy).
- **Convergence** - several capabilities hitting combinations of decisive points at once, so the
  enemy faces more problems than it can answer.
- **Endurance** - be able to keep going through the depth of the campaign (supply, reserves,
  replacements).
- **Depth** - extend the operation in time, space and purpose: hit their rear and their future,
  not just their front line.
- **Operational reach** - how far and how long the force can keep fighting effectively; beyond it
  the offensive stalls.

## Defeat mechanisms [81-83, 6-117+] - how the enemy is beaten, not just where
- **Destroy** - make it combat-ineffective. The threat of destruction powers the other three.
- **Dislocate** - gain positional advantage so their dispositions stop mattering (envelopments,
  turning movements, deception): they must accept losing part of their force or risk it moving.
- **Disintegrate** - break their command and coordination so the parts no longer act as a whole
  (in games: kill commanders/generals, cut command range, break cohesion).
- **Isolate** - separate them from support (supply, reinforcement, other units).
- Use more than one at once (multiple dilemmas). Total annihilation is rarely needed - enough to
  stop them reaching their objective.

## Operational framework [85-95]
- **Areas**: deep (beyond the fight, where you shape what arrives later), close (where the fight is
  decided), rear/support (where your sustainment lives - protect it).
- **Purposes**: decisive operation (achieves the mission), shaping operations (set conditions for
  it), sustaining operations (keep the force going).
- **Main effort** (the one unit or group most critical now; it gets priority) and **supporting
  efforts**. Shift the main effort deliberately, not by drift.
- **Zone** (offense: rear and lateral boundaries only) vs **sector** (defense: also interlocking fires).
- **Supporting range** (inside the other unit's weapon range) and **supporting distance** (can reach
  it in time to stop its defeat) - keep separated forces within one or the other.
- **Decisive point** [81] - terrain, event, factor or function that gives a marked advantage when
  acted on. Find them before choosing the main effort.

## Elements of operational art (ADP 3-0, referred to in FM 3-0 ch. 1)
End state and conditions - center of gravity (the source of their strength or will) - decisive
points (the ways to reach it) - lines of operation (physical routes from base to objective) and
lines of effort (logical chains of tasks) - tempo - phasing and transitions (plan the switch, e.g.
offense to defense before culmination) - culmination (the point where the force can no longer
continue; anticipate it) - operational reach - basing (where you project from) - risk.

## Relative advantage [36-37]
Physical (numbers, quality, position), information (understanding, decision speed, influence on
what they do), human (training, morale, leadership, will). Win by creating and exploiting a few of
these, not by being better at everything.

## Using it in a game turn
1. What is the **end state** of this war/campaign/battle (e.g. FoG II: rout 40% of theirs and 25% more
   than ours before turn 24)?
2. Their **center of gravity** and the **decisive points** that lead to it.
3. Which **defeat mechanism(s)** - destroy, dislocate, disintegrate, isolate?
4. **Main effort**, shaping and sustaining efforts; deep/close/rear.
5. **Phases** and the **transition** before culmination; **operational reach** (supply, ammo, turns).
6. Check against the principles of war; keep it simple.
