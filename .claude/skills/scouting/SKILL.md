# Scouting - knowing before committing

The user (2026-11-02, FoG II battle 2): I planned two "look" turns, but the whole French army was visible
from the start. In FoG II units are generally only hidden in forests. Fog of war will matter more in
TOAW IV. Scouting is about knowledge, not a fixed phase. If the board already gives the knowledge, take
it and use the time.

## What scouting is for
1. **Composition:** what the enemy has, by type, quality and count, and where its best units are.
2. **Positions:** who is where, which way they face, and the gaps and flanks.
3. **Intent:** where the strike units sit (that's where they mean to hit), what moved since last turn,
   and what is being held back.
4. **Ground:** terrain ahead of my moves, especially what the picture doesn't show (rough, streams,
   enclosures, dead ground, heights).
5. **Denial (counter-scouting):** keep the enemy from learning the same about me. Kill or drive off
   their scouts, and hide in the terrain the game lets units hide in.

## Doctrine underneath (military-doctrine skill, FM 3-90 ch. 12)
Recon orients on the enemy and the ground; security orients on my own force. FM 3-90's seven
recon fundamentals, as game rules: scout every turn, not just once; **never hold scouts back in
reserve**; know what question this scouting answers; report it to the plan straight away (board,
plan file); keep the scout able to get away; once contact is made, keep it; develop the situation
fast. Forms: route (one path), zone (everything between boundaries), area (one spot), recon in
force (attack to see how they react), recon by fire (shoot at where they might be).

## First question: what can be hidden in THIS game?
Look it up (manual-first) and write it in the game's scouting skill or guide. Then decide:
- **Nothing or almost nothing hidden:** no look phase. Read the board every turn instead (below).
- **Some places hide units** (woods, villages, dead ground): scout only those places, and only if
  something there could change the plan.
- **Real fog of war:** scouting is a standing job with its own units and its own share of the turn.
Weigh it against the clock. On a turn limit, a turn spent looking is a turn not spent winning.

## Without fog of war, scouting is reading
Every turn, before planning:
- Photograph the whole board (photo-map), and count and name both armies (hover the units).
- **Classify:** strike units (knights, cavalry), line units, shooters, lights, weak filler. Count
  each against mine. The difference says who should attack where.
- **Read intent:** strike units at the ends of a line mean a flank threat. A reserve behind the
  centre means a counter-punch. A thin wing means it's refused or bait.
- **Diff the turns:** what moved, how far and toward what. Movement reveals the plan.
- **Hiding places:** list every square where the game allows hiding (FoG II: forests) and decide
  whether one could matter.
- **Invisible ground:** hover the squares on my path before moving (the terrain tooltip). The
  picture lies about rough, streams and heights.
- **Threat ranges:** for each enemy strike unit, ask what it can reach next turn (move plus charge)
  and which of my units are inside that reach.

## With fog of war
- Assign recon units and give them their own moves each turn, at the edges and gaps of what I know.
- **Probe:** a small unit touching the enemy line shows what's there. Expect to lose some probes.
- **Last-known positions go stale.** Note the turn each sighting was made and how far the unit
  could have moved since.
- Read the game's reports and intelligence screens, the combat results, and the enemy's own moves.
- Assume the unseen enemy is where it would hurt most, until a scout says otherwise.

## Per game
Every game with a map keeps its scouting rules in its own skill or guide: what hides where, how to
see it (overlays, tooltips, LOS), which units scout, and what the turn clock allows.
- FoG II Medieval: fog2m-scouting (game folder).
- TOAW IV: fog of war is a real concern (the user). Learn its rules before the first battle.
- Dominions 6: detection is an **opposed roll** - patrol strength + 2d25 vs stealth strength + 2d25, both
  open-ended (commanders count double, unrest weakens patrols); independents never patrol; reports can be
  wrong about numbers, rarely about unit types (dom6-stealth). Pattern for any game: find the detection
  formula, compute the odds with dice-math, and park scouts where the odds are good.
