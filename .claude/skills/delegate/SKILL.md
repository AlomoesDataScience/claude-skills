# Acting as a delegate

**The user (2026-11-03, FoG II battle 2):** "You're going to be acting on your own, you need to be able to
handle this decision making on your own, I'm helping you now, so keep that in mind. You also want to write
a skill for acting as a delegate." And: "only commit when you are absolutely certain of a decision. You're
too hasty."

While the user helps, every correction he makes is a check I must learn to make myself. When he hands off,
nobody catches my mistakes - so I catch them, slowly, before acting.

## 1. Take the hand-off properly (mission command)
Write down, in the plan file, what was handed over:
- **Intent** - purpose, key tasks, end state, in his words ("break their line", "finish the battle").
- **Bounds** - what I may decide alone and what stays his. Standing rules always hold: End Turn only on his
  go unless he explicitly delegated it; no settings, network, purchases, publishing; game-specific rules.
- **Accepted risk** - what he is willing to lose for the goal (a unit, time, a turn).
- **Report points** - when he wants to look (e.g. "I want to review the beginning of the next turn").
Doctrine: commander's intent + disciplined initiative = act on the intent when the situation changes, never
outside it (military-doctrine; FM 3-0 mission command).

## 2. The self-review - ask the user's questions myself
Before each turn or phase, answer these in writing. Each one is a correction he had to make:
- **Every unit**: does it have a job, and did it use its AP for that job? ("Is that what you want them all to do?")
- **Rules**: did I check the manual for the mechanic I'm relying on? ("check the manual", "check that with the rules")
- **Facing**: where does each unit face at the end, and is that what the next turn needs? (turns cost AP)
- **Lanes and order**: who must move first so others can pass? ("one infantry one space, then knights, then the rest")
- **Behind the target**: what enemy sits behind it, where does a win's pursuit end?
- **When charged**: what happens to each exposed unit if it is attacked - facing, evade, fall back, who can reach it?
- **Flanks**: is every flank covered or deliberately accepted?
- **Pursuit / ZOC / special moves**: which rules let enemies ignore my blocks (pursuers ignore ZOC)?
- **Combined arms**: is the line supporting the strike force (hammer and anvil)? Are screens forcing reactions?
- **Time**: turns left versus what the plan needs.

## 3. The certainty test (before ANY irreversible action)
Commit only when ALL are true:
1. The plan for this action is written and fits the intent.
2. Geometry, costs and reach are checked on the screen (not from memory).
3. The game's own forecast is read (odds, casualties, costs).
4. The failure case is known and acceptable (what if it loses, falls back, evades, gets caught?).
5. No newer information contradicts it (re-read the board after the last action).
If any is false: not certain.

## 4. When not certain
In this order: look it up (manual, guide, wiki) -> get the forecast -> pick the reversible or safer option
(moves can often be undone; charges and shots can't) -> hold and note why -> ask the user only if the choice is
genuinely his (goals, risk he hasn't accepted, bounds). Never fill a gap with a guess that commits.
A message that might be a question is a question. Never chain "forecast" and "commit" in one script.

## 5. Act slowly, one step at a time
One order, check the result on the screen, update the picture, then the next. Re-plan when something
surprises (a unit falls back, an enemy moves). Speed is not a goal; being right is.

## 6. Decision log and hand-back
Keep a short log in the plan file: decision, reason, what happened. When handing back (report point, end of
the delegated task): lead with the situation in his terms, then what I did and why, then mistakes and what I
learned (into the skills), then the open decisions that are his. Never claim a result I didn't verify.
