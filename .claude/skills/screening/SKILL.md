# Screening - make the enemy answer you first

The user (2026-11-02): "Screening is where you have a unit in front that forces the enemy to react to it.
It's not strong enough to win a battle if it happens, but the enemy has to respect it. The point is,
now the enemy cannot simply plan around your advancing line without any problems, and has to think
about responding to your raiding party or any unit that is threatening first or losing units like
their own screens."

## What a screen does
- **Forces a reaction.** It threatens something the enemy cares about: shooting into a flank,
  reaching their shooters or lights, raiding the rear. Ignoring it costs them, so they spend moves,
  units or attention on it.
- **Costs them tempo.** Every enemy move spent on my screen is a move not spent on my main line.
- **Delays and pins.** A unit facing a threat can't freely turn or march away. Units that turn toward
  the screen open other angles.
- **Covers my main body.** It keeps enemy skirmishers and scouts off my line, and takes the first
  contact so the line arrives intact.
- **Hides and confuses.** The enemy sees the screen first, not what's behind it.
- **Kills enemy screens.** Where theirs are out, mine fight them so their line goes forward blind
  and unprotected.

## What makes a screen work
1. **A real threat.** If it could never hurt them, they'll ignore it. Missile troops, fast units and
   anything that can reach a flank or a weak unit all qualify.
2. **It can get away.** It's faster than what will chase it, or it can evade or fall back by the
   game's rules. A screen caught by heavy troops is just a lost unit.
3. **Support.** The main body is close enough that chasing the screen brings the chaser near my line
   or my counter-punch. That turns their reaction into my opportunity.
4. **Not isolated.** It shouldn't stand so far out that it can be cut off or surrounded. Check the
   enemy's move-plus-charge reach every turn (scouting).
5. **The right place.** On the enemy's approach routes, opposite their weak or slow units, or on the
   flank they need. Not in front of my own shooters' line of fire.

## Running a screen turn by turn
- Start each turn by asking what the screen threatens now and what can catch it next turn.
- Advance it ahead of the line, but stay outside reach of their strike units. If I can't avoid that,
  make sure it can evade.
- Make it harass: shoot, threaten and stay. Its job is the reaction, not the kill.
- **Pull it back** when the lines are about to meet. It clears the front, moving to a flank or behind
  the line, so it doesn't block my own troops or get trapped between them.
- Count it as a success when the enemy moved because of it, even if it hit nothing.

## Counter-screening
Their screen wants my reaction. Answer with the cheapest unit that solves it: my own lights, or
shooting. Don't turn the main line, and don't send strike units chasing lights they can't catch.

## Per game
- **FoG II Medieval:** light horse and light foot are the screens. Lights can evade charges from
  heavier troops (check the evade rules in the manual before relying on it). Their shooting forces
  cohesion tests. Battle 2: the Light Javelin Horse (20 AP) screens the southern lane, the Light
  Archers the northern one. Ahead of the spear line they make the French knights choose between
  chasing them and holding.
- Other games: write each game's screen units and escape rules in its guide.
