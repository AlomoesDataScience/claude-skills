# Soviet doctrine for games

Sources (Military Theory shelf; Claude's own summary):
- `FM 100-2-1(84).pdf` + `.txt` - US Army's 1984 description of Soviet operations and tactics
  (scanned with an OCR layer; references are manual sections, e.g. "2-10").
- `CIA-RDP82-00047R000200530003-7.pdf` + `.txt` - CIA information report (Feb 1953) on the
  Soviet Armed Forces Field Manual 1948 (PU-48). Typewriter scan, OCR'd by Claude: readable but
  with letter errors ("Seviet", e/o swaps).

## Big ideas (FM 100-2-1 ch. 2)
- **Laws of war** (Sokolovsky): be prepared - forces in being decide the opening; the side that can
  sustain a long war wins; the higher the stakes, the longer and more violent; the war must be seen
  as just. In short: whoever reaches the first battle with the most wins.
- **Principles of military art**: readiness; surprise, decisiveness, seize and hold the initiative;
  use every asset; continuous coordination of all arms; pick the main objective and the best route,
  and **concentrate decisively at the right time**; destroy the enemy's forces rather than chase
  geography; give the enemy more than one problem at once; keep him reacting to situations you
  control; hit weak points, not strengths; detailed preparation before the attack. 1970s list:
  mobility and high tempo, concentration at the decisive place and time, surprise and security,
  combat activeness, preserve own combat power, goals that fit the real situation, coordination.
- **Offense is the normal form of combat.** Defense is temporary (to gain time, regroup, economise
  for an attack elsewhere) and must turn into offense when it can (PU-48 accepted defense as a
  legitimate form only after 1941).

## Echelons and ratios (2-10, 2-11)
- **First echelon** - attacks, has the mission now. **Second echelon** - has an assigned follow-on
  mission (exploit, continue the attack, take over from a spent first echelon). **Reserve** - no
  pre-assigned mission; the commander's tool for the unexpected (combined-arms reserve to exploit,
  small reserve ~1/9 for security and counterattacks, special reserves: antitank, engineer).
- Force ratio: about **3:1 overall** for an attack; historically 3-5:1 tanks, 6-8:1 artillery, 4-5:1
  men at the main attack. At the FEBA the visible edge may be only 2:1 - the rest is coming in depth.
  Create 3:1 at each level by choosing objectives.
- **Norms** (standard distances, rates, times) make drills fast and predictable; initiative is
  expected inside them. PU-48 too: formations are basic patterns, never to be used blindly.

## Attack (ch. 4-5)
- **Attack from the march** (preferred): deploy from march column at set control lines, attack
  formation about 1 km from the enemy - surprise, momentum, less exposure; harder coordination.
  Attack from contact is the fallback (usually when switching from defense).
- **Forms of maneuver**: frontal attack (least preferred alone - used with another form), **flank
  attack** (shallow, through gaps, coordinated with the frontal attack), **envelopment** (deeper,
  forces the enemy to turn and fight in a new direction; no need for mutual fire support).
- Breach at weak points or gaps; bypass strongpoints; mass fires; maneuver against flanks and rear;
  strike fast and deep; keep momentum under all conditions.
- **Forward detachments** and the **Operational Maneuver Group** (OMG): a strong mobile force that
  goes through the breach early and deep into the rear (4-5, 4-7, 4-9) - deep battle.
- Mechanised units sit in the second echelon and exploit once the infantry takes the first lines
  (PU-48): first phase to the enemy regimental reserves, then through the second defensive zone.

## PU-48 (1948 manual, per the CIA report)
- **Five combat formations** (regiments of a division): **wedge** (one forward, two back - the
  frontal attack draws their reserves to the centre, then the rear regiments hit both flanks and
  encircle); **inverted wedge** (two forward on the flanks, one back - when the centre is strong:
  flanks first, then the rear regiment breaks the centre, splitting them in two); **staggered right
  / left** (each regiment behind and to one side - for attacking a flank); **line** (all abreast, no
  second echelon - against a weak, unprepared, demoralised or retreating enemy and in pursuit).
- **Encircling battle** = the necessary last phase of a major offensive: hold them tight, stop
  relief and breakout, then annihilate or starve; spare forces exploit in the rear.
- **Artillery is the "Queen of Battle"**; infantry is the main arm that takes and holds ground,
  with all other arms attached to it; tanks exploit; recon is the commander's "eyes and ears";
  "liaison is the nerve system of the army".

## Using it in games
- Facing a Soviet-style opponent (or AI): expect echelons - the first attack is not the last;
  keep your reserve for the second echelon and the exploitation force, watch for the deep thrust.
- Playing it: concentrate for 3:1 at one point while holding elsewhere thinly; attack from the move;
  feed the second echelon into success, not failure; turn every breakthrough into an encirclement.
- FoG II example: the **wedge** maps onto "fix the centre, then strike both flanks"; the **staggered**
  formation onto our south-flank envelopment (line on the left, knights stepped back on the right).
