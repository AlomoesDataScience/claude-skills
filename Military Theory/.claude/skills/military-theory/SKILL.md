# Military theory shelf

Status 2026-11-01: all texts searchable (`.txt` next to each PDF; Gutenberg `pg*.txt` are text
already). Summaries are Claude's own words; quote the books sparingly.

## the user's own work (his perspective comes first in our designs)

**On the Nature of Space Warfare** (`On the Nature of Space Warfare.txt`, ~1200 words):
- Space is empty: fighting happens near bodies or on transfers between them. Lasers keep going
  and spread; nukes spray radiation everywhere.
- Area weapons are both critical and useless: precise tracking makes a direct-fire system beat
  a dumb area weapon fired blind.
- Armour is beaten by any serious weapon today. The real defence is not being hit: **electronic
  warfare (E-War) and stealth** to degrade targeting, **point defence** to intercept, dodging
  only against low-intensity attacks. Stealth does not stop tracking: everyone knows where
  everything is (asteroid paranoia), but nobody knows what anyone is *doing* until they check.
- Today nobody defends in space: a total space war kills everything in orbit at once, so the
  likely wars are low-intensity and cyber (compromising satellites, jamming). The force that
  wages it looks like a lab of radio, EMP and hacking specialists (Stargate, not Star Trek); the
  missile crews just keep the stockpile.
- Interstellar war takes 20+ years per trip, so it is planned far ahead; each colony is like
  another dimension (Spelljammer, the Forgotten Realms treatment). The first thing to master
  is an indefinitely sustainable space station.
- Diplomacy (arms limits, no-weapons treaties) is a defence layer, but it only shifts the game
  to balance of power. History doesn't end; plan for a space war now.
- In the 4X design docs: planet-launched missiles are overpowered; early FTL total war = FTL
  drives strapped to missiles (extermination, so rare); a real system war needs bases in the
  system, replenishment and areas of denial. See also his "Eras of Space War" (Projects/Our Games/4x
  Game Engine) and the WMD mod's eras.

## The classics (files and core ideas)

- **Clausewitz, On War** (`pg1946.txt`): war continues policy by other means; friction and
  fog; the culminating point of the attack; defence as the stronger form; centres of gravity;
  the trinity of passion, chance and reason (people, army, government).
- **Jomini, The Art of War** (`pg13549.txt`; `The Art of War.pdf` is a scanned translation of
  the same book): principles and lines of operation, interior lines, concentration of mass at
  the decisive point, bases and communications.
- **Corbett, Some Principles of Maritime Strategy** (`pg15076.txt`): command of the sea is
  about controlling communications, usually in dispute; fleet in being; limited war; sea
  control vs **sea denial**. Directly fits the user's "areas of denial" in a solar system.
- **Napoleon's Maxims** (`pg50750.txt`): short rules on concentration, speed, unity of command.
- **Roosevelt, The Naval War of 1812** (`pg9104.txt`): ship-by-ship history; quality,
  gunnery and training against numbers.
- **Mao, On Guerrilla Warfare** (FMFRP 12-18, Griffith translation, PDF + txt): guerrillas need
  the people's support, avoid strength and strike weakness, protracted war in stages toward
  regular war. Fits the engine's rebellions and "collapse into independent nations".
- Not on the shelf yet: Sun Tzu (the "Art of War" PDF is Jomini's).

## Uses in our games

Space combat (E-War/stealth/PD layers, tracking vs targeting), sea-control/denial for
system-scale war, logistics and culmination (OCS supply, Clausewitz), insurgency and rebellion
(Mao), AI doctrines (Jomini's principles are easy to script). Keep the user's essay as the default
view and the classics as support.
